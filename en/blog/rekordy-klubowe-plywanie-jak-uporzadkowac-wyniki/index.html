Swimming Club Records: Why a Results Table Is Not Enough

Publishing swimming records requires rules for age categories, pool lengths and trustworthy source data.

A deceptively simple task

A club records page needs more than a list of fast times. Every result must be tied to an event, pool length, age category and the correct athlete and club.

Where results come from

Swimrankings.net provides swimming results and competition information. Data still needs checking, grouping and presentation according to each club's rules.

Dozens of record boards

Separate categories for gender, 25-metre and 50-metre pools and age groups can produce around forty boards in a typical example. One spreadsheet becomes hard to navigate.

Different clubs, different categories

A small club may not have results in every age group. The interface should reflect categories that actually exist rather than assume every club uses an identical structure.

Can younger swimmers break older-category records?

A younger swimmer might outperform a record in an older category. Whether it counts depends on the club's eligibility rules, which the system must apply consistently.

What does a blank record mean?

An empty position might mean no eligible result, incomplete source data or a category-specific restriction. Those cases should not be confused.

Why Excel is not enough

Spreadsheets are useful for data preparation but awkward for public browsing, especially on phones at competitions.

Filters make the difference

An anonymised Club Records screenshot shows filters for club, gender, pool length and age. Users can find the relevant board instead of browsing dozens of sheets.

The Club Records application

My public application lets visitors select a club and filter results. The design considerations described here are not all claims about features already implemented.

Conclusion

The challenge is defining reliable rules and helping visitors reach the correct record, not simply displaying more tables.
