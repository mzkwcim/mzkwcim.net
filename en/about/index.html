From a real problem to a useful tool

My process: I review the club's existing data and workflow, clarify requirements and constraints, build the smallest useful solution and check failures and maintainability. I choose tools after understanding the problem.

I start with concrete needs: training plans, attendance, swimming records, membership fees and reports. Understanding how the work is done comes before choosing technology.

I prioritize clear data, straightforward workflows and tools that can be maintained without unnecessary complexity. I also share practical lessons in the blog, whether the right answer is a spreadsheet, automation or an application.

REAL-WORLD EXPERIENCE

Problems in the pool come before technology

An attendance percentage alone does not show which training sessions or exercises an athlete actually completed. Two athletes can have similar attendance while missing very different types of work. This is one of the ideas behind Swimtopia, a project focused on training plans and making sense of their execution.

Club Records came from another practical problem. Swimrankings contains swimming results, but presenting records for a particular club or regional organization means navigating different ages, genders and pool lengths. A readable public interface makes that information easier to explore.

Reporting was another source of repetitive work. In Poznań, as many as ten coaches worked on MCS reports. With approximately 120 athletes, many competitions and places to verify, one spreadsheet could take several evenings to prepare. That experience motivated me to automate the process.

HOW I WORK

Understanding the workflow comes first

I start by understanding who uses a process, where its data comes from and which steps are repetitive or error-prone. Only then do I consider an appropriate solution and how it will be maintained.

Not every problem requires a large application. Sometimes a repeatable report is enough. In other cases, a tool such as Club Dues can help manage attendance and membership fees, while a public records site serves an entirely different audience.

I share the reasoning behind these projects on the blog, focusing on practical problems rather than publishing every implementation detail.
